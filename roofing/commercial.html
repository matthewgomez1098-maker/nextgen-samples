Comparing your low-slope options

Flat roofs aren't truly flat. They slope gently toward drains and scuppers, and the right membrane depends on the building, the foot traffic on the roof and how long you plan to own it. We'll walk the roof with you and recommend one option, with the others priced alongside it.

TPO single-ply membrane

TPO is a white, reflective sheet that comes in wide rolls. Seams are heat-welded with hot air, so they fuse into one continuous membrane. The white surface reflects a lot of the sun, which helps keep the top floor cooler in an Inland Empire summer.

It's a good fit for warehouses, big-box retail and most new low-slope roofs. It can be mechanically fastened or fully adhered over new insulation board, which is also a chance to improve drainage with tapered insulation.

  • Pros: reflective, heat-welded seams, fast to install on large roofs
  • Cons: can puncture under heavy foot traffic without walk pads
  • Best for: large open roofs, buildings where cooling costs matter

Modified bitumen

Modified bitumen is an asphalt-based membrane installed in layers, usually with a granulated cap sheet on top. It's thick and tough, and handles foot traffic around rooftop HVAC units well.

It's common on older commercial and multifamily buildings, and on smaller roofs with lots of penetrations. Cap sheets come in white or can be coated to reflect more heat.

  • Pros: tough, multi-layer, easy to patch
  • Cons: heavier, darker unless coated, more seams than single-ply
  • Best for: roofs with heavy foot traffic, lots of equipment or small, cut-up sections

Roof coatings: restore instead of replace

If your existing roof is still mostly dry and sound, a fluid-applied coating can add years of life for a fraction of the cost of a tear-off. We repair seams and flashing, clean the roof, reinforce problem areas, then spray or roll on silicone or acrylic coating.

Before we recommend a coating, we check for trapped moisture. Coating over wet insulation just seals the problem in, so if the roof is saturated we'll tell you.

  • Little to no tear-off, less noise and debris
  • Bright white finish that reflects heat
  • Can be recoated again later

Maintenance plans, inspections and reports

Most commercial roof leaks start small: a clogged drain, a split seam at an HVAC curb, a loose counterflashing. A maintenance plan catches them before they reach the ceiling tiles.

Every inspection includes a written report with drone photos, a roof map with problems marked, and each item ranked as urgent, soon or monitor. Property managers get a PDF they can file or forward to owners.

  • Inspections twice a year, usually before and after the rainy season
  • Drains, scuppers and gutters cleared
  • Seams, flashing and penetrations checked and sealed
  • Priority response for leaks between visits
  • Remaining-life estimate to help you budget for replacement

Questions about commercial

Should we replace or coat our roof?

It depends on how much of the roof is wet underneath. We do a moisture survey and core samples first, then recommend the option that makes sense.

Can you work while our building is open?

Yes. Most commercial jobs are done with the building fully open. We schedule noisy or smelly work around your hours.

Do you work with property managers and HOAs?

Yes. We provide written reports, photos and line-item proposals that are easy to pass along for approval.

How often should a flat roof be inspected?

We suggest twice a year and after any major storm.
