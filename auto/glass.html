Chip or crack: repair or replace?

A small chip can often be filled with resin so it doesn't spread. The repair takes about 30 minutes and restores the strength of the glass. The chip won't disappear, but it usually becomes much harder to see.

Chips grow with heat, cold and bumps. A hot afternoon followed by the AC on full can turn a chip into a crack across the glass. The sooner you get it filled, the better the chance we can save the windshield.

  • Usually repairable: a chip smaller than a quarter
  • Usually repairable: a short crack, a few inches long
  • Replace: damage right in the driver's line of sight
  • Replace: cracks that reach the edge of the glass
  • Replace: damage over the camera area at the top of the windshield

How we replace a windshield

A windshield is part of your car's structure. It helps the roof stay up in a rollover and gives the passenger airbag something to push against. How it's installed matters as much as the glass itself.

  • We cover the hood, dash and seats
  • Remove the wipers, cowl and trim, then cut out the old glass
  • Trim the old adhesive and prime the frame to prevent rust
  • Set the new glass with fresh adhesive, centered on the frame
  • Reinstall the trim, wipers, mirror and sensors
  • Clean up, check for leaks and set the safe drive-away time

Safe drive-away time

The adhesive that holds the windshield needs time to set before the car is safe to drive. That time depends on the adhesive, the temperature and the humidity. For most jobs it's about an hour, and we'll tell you the exact time before we leave or hand you the keys.

  • Leave the retention tape on for 24 hours
  • Crack a window slightly for the first day so air pressure doesn't push on fresh glass
  • Skip the car wash for two days
  • Avoid slamming the doors for the first day

Camera recalibration

Many newer cars have a camera at the top of the windshield. It reads lane lines and road signs and helps with automatic emergency braking. When the windshield is replaced, the camera has to be recalibrated so it sees the road correctly.

We check what your car's manufacturer requires. Some cars calibrate in the shop with a target board. Others calibrate during a drive on marked roads. You get a printed report when it's done. Recalibration is done in the shop, so cars that need it can't be finished with mobile service.

Mobile glass service

We can repair chips and replace most windshields at your home or work across our service area. All we need is a level place to park and a few feet of space around the car. If it's raining, we'll reschedule or ask for a covered spot.

Mobile service is free within our service area. If your car needs a camera recalibration, we'll schedule the job at the shop instead.

Questions about glass

Will insurance cover my windshield?

Many policies with comprehensive coverage include glass. Coverage and deductibles vary, so call your insurer or let us check the claim for you.

Do you use new windshields?

Yes. Every windshield we install is new glass made to fit your car, including the brackets for cameras and sensors.

How long does a replacement take?

About an hour to install, plus the safe drive-away time. Add an hour or two if your camera needs recalibration.

Can you fix a chip in the rain?

In the shop, yes. For mobile jobs, we need the glass dry, so we'll reschedule or find a covered spot.
