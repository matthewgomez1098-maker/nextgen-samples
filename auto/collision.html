Working with your insurance

We work with every major insurer. Start the claim with your insurance company, then give them our name and number. We'll send them our estimate and photos and handle the back and forth with the adjuster.

Your insurer may suggest a shop from their network. You can still have your car repaired here. Ask your adjuster how that works on your policy, and call us if you have questions. You pay your deductible when you pick up the car.

  • Your claim number
  • The adjuster's name and phone or email
  • Photos from the accident scene, if you have them
  • Your deductible amount

Frame and structural repair

Modern cars are built with a unibody, where the body and frame are one structure designed to absorb a crash in a specific way. A hard hit can shift that structure even when the outside looks fine. The signs are uneven panel gaps, doors that don't close right and a car that won't align.

We measure the structure against the manufacturer's specifications, pull it back into place and replace parts that can't be straightened. We follow the manufacturer's repair procedures for welds, adhesives and which parts must be replaced instead of repaired.

Paint that matches

Your car's paint code tells us the factory color. Sun, age and the plant where the car was built all change the exact shade. We spray test cards, compare them to your car in daylight and adjust the tint before painting a single panel.

To hide the edge of a repair, we blend the new color into the panels next to it. The finish is cured in our heated paint booth, then wet sanded and polished to match the factory texture.

  • Wait about 30 days before waxing new paint
  • Hand wash for the first few weeks and skip the automatic car wash
  • Wash bird droppings and tree sap off quickly

Safety system calibration after repairs

Many cars have cameras and radar sensors behind the windshield, grille, bumpers and mirrors. They run features like automatic emergency braking, lane keeping and adaptive cruise. After a collision repair, those sensors may need to be recalibrated so they aim where they should.

We check the manufacturer's requirements for your car after every repair. Some calibrations are done in the shop with targets, and some on a road drive. You get a printed report showing the calibration was completed.

Rental cars and how long it takes

Repair time depends on the damage and how fast parts arrive. A bumper repair is often two to three days. Larger jobs with structural work can take one to three weeks. Back-ordered parts are the most common cause of delays, and we'll tell you as soon as we know.

If your policy includes rental coverage, we'll help set it up so the rental is ready when you drop off. You get photo updates by text at teardown, during repair, after paint and before pickup.

Questions about collision

Do I need more than one estimate?

Your insurer will tell you what they require. Many accept one estimate from the shop you choose.

Is the collision repair covered by your warranty?

Yes. Our 24-month / 24,000-mile warranty covers parts and labor on collision repairs too.

Can I drive the car in for an estimate?

If it's safe to drive, yes. If lights are broken, fluids are leaking or the wheel is damaged, have it towed in.

Do you use new parts?

We'll list the type of each part on your estimate. If your policy allows a choice, we'll explain the options.
