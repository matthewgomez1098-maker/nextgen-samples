Leaks you can see, and leaks you can't

A dripping faucet is easy to spot. A pinhole leak inside a wall or under the slab can run for months before you notice. We find leaks with pressure testing, moisture meters and acoustic listening gear, so we open as little drywall as possible.

Once we find the source, we show you what we found and give you a price to fix it. Small repairs are usually done the same visit. Bigger jobs like a slab leak reroute get scheduled with a written plan.

  • Your water meter spins when every tap is off
  • Warm spots on a tile or concrete floor
  • Stains, bubbling paint or a musty smell on a wall
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on
  • Low pressure at one fixture or the whole house

Drain cleaning and clogs

One slow sink is usually a local clog of hair, soap or grease. Several slow drains at once, or water backing up into the tub when you flush, points to the main sewer line. In older Inland Empire neighborhoods, tree roots in clay sewer pipe are a common cause.

We clear clogs with a cable machine or a hydro-jetter, depending on the pipe and what's in it. For repeat main-line clogs, we run a camera through the line and show you the video so you can see roots, sags or breaks for yourself.

Water heaters: tank or tankless

A standard tank water heater lasts about 8 to 12 years. Hard water shortens that, because mineral scale builds up in the bottom of the tank. Rust-colored water, popping or rumbling sounds, and water pooling around the base all mean it's time to look at a replacement.

Tank heaters cost less up front and are quick to swap. Tankless units heat water on demand, take up less space and don't run out of hot water, but they cost more to install and may need a larger gas line or new venting. We'll look at your setup and give you both prices side by side.

Every replacement includes removing the old unit, new connectors and shutoff valve, seismic strapping, the permit and haul-away.

Repiping

Many homes built from the 1950s through the 1970s still have galvanized steel pipe. It corrodes from the inside, which causes low pressure, brown water and leaks that keep coming back. Some homes with copper pipe also develop repeated pinhole leaks over time.

A whole-home repipe replaces the supply lines with new PEX or copper. Most homes are done in two to four days, and you have running water at the end of each work day. We patch the drywall openings when we're done, and we check what your city requires for permits and inspections.

  • Free in-home estimate with a written scope
  • Furniture and floors covered before we cut
  • Water on each night during the job
  • Drywall patch included, paint-ready

Fixtures and small jobs

We install and repair faucets, toilets, garbage disposals, shower valves, hose bibs, angle stops, pressure regulators and water filtration systems. You can supply your own fixture, or we can bring a reliable mid-range option.

If you have a list of small plumbing items, book them together. One visit costs less than three separate ones.

  • Running toilet or weak flush
  • Leaking or loose faucet
  • Disposal that hums but won't turn
  • Shutoff valves that won't close all the way
  • Pressure regulator replacement for high or banging pressure

Plumbing emergencies

Burst pipes, sewage backups and water heaters leaking onto the floor can't wait. Call us and a person answers. We'll get a plumber headed your way and talk you through stopping the water while you wait.

Know where your main shutoff is before you need it. In most Southern California homes it's near the front hose bib or on the outside wall closest to the street. If you can't find yours, our tech will show you on any visit.

  • Turn off the main water shutoff or the valve at the fixture
  • For a leaking water heater, close the cold supply valve on top
  • Turn off power to any outlets near standing water
  • Move rugs and boxes out of the wet area
  • Take photos for your insurance before cleanup starts
