Speakers and tuning

Factory speakers are built to a price. Most use thin paper cones and small magnets mounted in plastic doors that vibrate along with the music. An upgrade starts with the front stage, because that's where your ears expect the sound to come from.

Component speakers separate the tweeter from the midrange woofer. We mount tweeters high, in the sail panel or dash, so vocals sit at eye level in front of you. Coaxial speakers keep both drivers in one unit and are a clean, budget-friendly fit for rear doors.

Then we tune. A digital signal processor lets us set crossovers, time alignment and equalization for each speaker. Time alignment delays the closer speakers so sound from both sides reaches you at the same moment. The result is a centered stage, like the band is playing on your dashboard.

  • Components: separate tweeter and woofer for the best imaging
  • Coaxials: all-in-one, simple to install, good for rear fill
  • Custom mounting rings so speakers seal tight to the door
  • Tuning with a measurement mic after every install

Subwoofers and amplifiers

A subwoofer handles the low notes your door speakers can't reproduce. The enclosure matters as much as the sub. We build boxes to fit your trunk, cargo area or under a truck's rear seat, then match the amplifier to the sub's power handling.

Amplifiers give speakers clean power so they stay clear at higher volume. We size the power wire to the amp, fuse it near the battery, and ground it to bare metal on the chassis. For bigger systems, we look at the charging system and may suggest a battery or wiring upgrade.

  • Sealed: tight, accurate bass, smaller box, great for all music
  • Ported: louder and deeper on the low notes, needs more space
  • Bandpass: very loud in a narrow range, best for bass-heavy music
  • Under-seat and stealth boxes: keep your trunk or cab space

Head units, CarPlay and Android Auto

A new head unit adds wireless or wired Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, a bigger touchscreen, and better sound settings. We use dash kits made for your car so the screen fits flush and the trim matches.

We keep the features you already use. Steering wheel buttons, backup cameras, factory USB ports and many factory amplifiers stay working through interface modules. On cars where the factory screen is built into the dash, we can often add CarPlay to the original screen.

  • Wireless CarPlay and Android Auto
  • Steering wheel controls retained
  • Factory or new backup camera connected
  • Pre-amp outputs for adding amplifiers later

Sound deadening

A car door is a thin metal shell full of holes. When a speaker plays, the door panel vibrates and rattles, and bass leaks out through the openings. Sound deadening fixes that.

We apply damping mats to the outer and inner door skins, seal the large access holes, and add foam where plastic trim touches metal. You get tighter midbass, fewer rattles and less road noise. Many customers notice the quieter ride before they notice the music. Trunks, floors and roofs can be treated too.

Lighting and window tint

Ambient interior lighting runs fiber-optic strips along the dash and doors, controlled from your phone. You choose the color and brightness, and you can set it to change with the music. We route every wire behind trim, so the install leaves nothing visible.

We also upgrade interior and exterior bulbs to LED, and install window tint that cuts heat and glare on San Gabriel Valley summer days. We keep tint within California's legal limits and can explain them in the shop.

Backup cameras and dash cams

Backup cameras can be added to almost any car or truck and shown on a new head unit, a mirror display or your factory screen. We mount the camera at the license plate or tailgate handle and run the wire inside the car.

Dash cams record front and rear while you drive. We hardwire them to the fuse box so there's no cord hanging from the windshield. Parking mode can keep recording while the car is off, with a low-voltage cutoff so it won't drain your battery.

  • Backup, front and side cameras
  • Front and rear dash cams, hardwired
  • Parking mode with battery protection

Questions about systems

Do I need an amplifier for new speakers?

Not always. Many speakers sound better on factory power. An amp adds clarity at higher volume and is a must for a subwoofer.

Will a sub rattle my car?

Loose trim and license plates can buzz with bass. We deaden and secure the trunk area during the install and fix any rattles you find afterward.

Can you keep my factory radio?

Yes. We can add a processor or line output converter to your factory radio and build the system around it.

What is a DSP?

A digital signal processor sits between the radio and the amplifiers. It controls each speaker's timing, frequency range and EQ so the system can be tuned to your car.
